Mote de Queso

A thick, creamy Cartagena classic, ñame simmered until tender and finished with a sofrito and melted cheese. One of the most beloved soups of the Colombian Caribbean coast.

PREP 10 min COOK 55 min SERVES 4
Servings
4
3 lbs ñame
Ingredients
    No queso costeño in the US? Feta is the closest match for flavor and crumble, and it's what most Colombian home cooks in the States reach for. Cotija and queso blanco are good options too. Some supermarkets and Sam's Club locations carry a Central American cheese called "queso El Viajero," which is very close to the real thing if you can track it down. A Latin market is your best bet for any of these.
    No suero either? That's fine, this version doesn't need it. Traditional versions sometimes finish with a drizzle of suero costeño (a tangy cultured cream) on top, but a good squeeze of fresh lime juice at the end does the same job of brightening the soup. If you want something closer to that tang, thin a little sour cream with milk and a squeeze of lime.
    Can't find ñame? Yuca or a regular yam both work as substitutes. Frozen ñame at a Latin market is often more reliable than fresh, and easier to work with, two bags of frozen is roughly equal to one large fresh ñame.
    Meal prep & storage ⌄

    Make ahead, reheats well, though the texture is best the day it's made.

    Store, airtight container in the fridge for up to 3 days.

    Reheat, stovetop over low heat, adding a splash of water if it's thickened up too much.
